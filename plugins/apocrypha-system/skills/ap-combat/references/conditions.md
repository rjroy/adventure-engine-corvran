# Stress Conditions

Apocrypha's consequence system targets keywords, not hit points. Stress conditions describe the narrative and mechanical state of a keyword under pressure.

---

## Light Stress

A keyword with light stress is shaken but still functional.

**Mechanical effect**: -1 to the keyword's effective modifier.

**Narrative expression**: The capability is unreliable, hesitant, or painful to use. The character can still invoke it, but something is off.

**Examples**:
- "Scarred Veteran (+2)" with light stress: The veteran flinches at sudden movements. Effective +1.
- "Silver-tongued when Cornered (+1)" with light stress: The words come, but the confidence behind them wavers. Effective +0.

**Clearing**:
- Rest (a scene of downtime: making camp, visiting a tavern, a quiet moment)
- Spending 1 Hope

**Sources**:
- GM spends 1 Fear
- Failure consequence narrated by GM (no Fear cost)
- Adversary Fear ability

---

## Deep Stress

A keyword with deep stress is seriously compromised. Something fundamental about the character's relationship to that capability is shaken.

**Mechanical effect**: -2 to the keyword's effective modifier.

**Narrative expression**: The capability is dangerous to use, unreliable in critical moments, or actively working against the character. Using the keyword at deep stress should feel like a risk.

**Examples**:
- "Pyromancer's Fury (+2)" with deep stress: The fire is uncontrolled, volatile. Flames leap where they shouldn't. Effective +0.
- "Commander of the Thornwall Garrison (+3)" with deep stress: The commands come, but they carry the weight of the soldiers who died following them. Effective +1.

**Clearing**:
- Only through narrative resolution: a scene that directly addresses the source of the stress
- The player and GM agree when the scene has adequately resolved the stress
- Requires a genuine narrative scene, not a passing mention
- Deep stress resolution is collaborative judgment

**Sources**:
- GM spends 2 Fear
- Major failure consequence narrated by GM
- Adversary Fear ability

---

## Combined Stress (Light + Deep)

A keyword can carry both light and deep stress simultaneously for a total of -3.

**Mechanical effect**: -3 to the keyword's effective modifier.

**Narrative expression**: The capability is at its absolute limit. A keyword at +1 with combined stress has effective modifier -2. The keyword is actively working against the character.

**Example**: "Pyromancer's Fury (+1)" with both light and deep stress (effective -2): The fire doesn't just resist control; it actively burns the character, lashing out at allies, igniting things that shouldn't burn. Invoking it is desperation.

**Clearing**: Clear light stress first (rest or 1 Hope), then address deep stress through narrative resolution.

---

## Stress Cap

A keyword's stress caps at -3 (light + deep). When a maxed keyword would take additional stress, the GM targets a different keyword instead, narrating the connection in fiction:

"A fourth sword blow doesn't stress 'Cutthroat Reflexes' again; it breaks your arm, stressing 'Mechanical Wiz' because you can't tinker without both hands."

The GM spreads stress across the character's keyword set, using fiction to justify the connections.

---

## Crisis State

A character is in **crisis** when more than half their keywords have negative effective modifiers.

**Mechanical effect**: None. Crisis is a narrative signal, not a mechanical state with additional rules.

**Narrative expression**: The character is breaking down, overwhelmed, at their limit. The GM narrates this as a turning point.

**Death**: Death is always a player choice, never a mechanical inevitability. Crisis is the floor. The stress system creates pressure and narrative consequence, but the terminal state is collaborative.

---

## Adversary Stress

Adversaries take stress the same way: it targets specific keywords and reduces their effectiveness. The key differences:

### Minor Adversaries (Aggregate Tracking)

Stress is a single counter. The GM narrates which capability is degrading, but tracking is one number toward the stress threshold.

### Standard and Major Adversaries (Per-Keyword Tracking)

Stress is allocated to specific keywords. Each keyword degrades independently. Total stress across all keywords counts toward the threshold.

**Example**: A dragon with "Impenetrable Scales (+3)" at 2 stress fights with effective +1 on that keyword. The fiction changes: the scales are cracking, showing vulnerability.
